Military discounts are one of the most common standing discounts at US retailers, and they are usually available year-round rather than only around holidays. Still, the word "military" can mean different things to different stores. Before you count on a discount, it helps to know who the store includes, how it checks your status, and what it leaves out.

## Who usually qualifies

There is no single definition. Each retailer writes its own eligibility rules, and they can change. These are the groups most programs consider.

### Active duty service members

Nearly every military discount program includes people currently serving on active duty in the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force, or Coast Guard.

### National Guard and Reserve

Many programs include members of the National Guard and Reserve, though a few limit eligibility to periods of active service. Check the terms if you drill part-time.

### Veterans

Veteran eligibility varies the most. Some stores include all veterans with an honorable discharge. Others limit the discount to veterans who served in a particular period, or to retirees only. A store that says "military discount" without mentioning veterans may not include them, so ask or read the program page.

### Retirees

Military retirees are commonly eligible, and some programs list them separately from veterans because they hold a retiree ID card.

### Spouses and dependents

Some retailers extend the discount to military spouses and dependents, while others do not. When spouses are included, the store may require the service member's status to be verified first, or may ask the spouse to verify on their own using a dependent ID.

### Related groups

A few programs also include Gold Star families, first responders, or other public-service groups. These are store-specific, so treat them as a bonus rather than the norm.

## How verification commonly works

Retailers need to confirm your status, and the method usually depends on whether you are shopping in person or online.

### In-store ID checks

At a physical store, you typically show proof at the register. Commonly accepted documents include:

- A military ID card (active duty, reserve, retiree, or dependent)
- A Veteran Health Identification Card from the VA
- A Veteran ID Card issued by the VA
- A state driver's license or ID with a veteran designation
- A copy of discharge paperwork, such as a DD-214, at some stores

Not every store accepts every document. If you rely on a less common one, call ahead or check the program page. The [VA website](https://www.va.gov/) explains the federal veteran ID options and how to request them.

### Online verification services

Online, most retailers use a third-party verification service rather than checking documents themselves. ID.me and SheerID are two well-known examples. The process generally looks like this:

1. You select the military offer on the retailer's site and are sent to a verification form.
2. You enter your name, date of birth, branch, and service dates.
3. The service tries to confirm your status automatically against official records.
4. If that does not work, you may be asked to upload a document for review.
5. Once approved, the retailer links the discount to your account, and it may apply automatically at checkout or appear as a code.

With some services, you create an account and can reuse that verification at other participating stores. Some retailers also link online verification to their loyalty program so the discount carries over to in-store purchases.

### Re-verification

Verification does not always last forever. Some stores ask you to verify again after a set period, especially for active duty members whose status will eventually change. If your discount suddenly stops working, an expired verification is the first thing to check.

## Common exclusions

Military discounts almost always come with a list of items they do not cover. These are the exclusions shoppers run into most often.

- **Sale and clearance items.** Many stores only apply the discount to full-price merchandise.
- **Gift cards.** Gift card purchases are excluded at nearly every retailer.
- **Large appliances and big-ticket categories.** Home improvement and department stores often exclude major appliances, and sometimes electronics or certain brands.
- **Services.** Installation, delivery fees, protection plans, and rentals are commonly left out.
- **Third-party marketplace items.** On sites that sell products from outside sellers, the discount may only apply to items sold by the retailer itself.
- **Annual caps.** Some stores limit how much you can save in a calendar year. Once you reach the cap, the discount stops working until the next year.

Because exclusions vary so much, look at the program terms before a large purchase, not at the register.

## Stacking rules

"Stacking" means combining more than one discount on the same order. With military programs, stacking rules tend to be strict.

- Many stores do not allow the military discount to be combined with coupon codes, other percentage-off promotions, or employee pricing.
- Some apply the military discount after other markdowns, while others block it on anything already discounted.
- Store credit card offers, loyalty rewards, and price matching each have their own rules about combining with military pricing.

When you have a choice between two discounts that cannot be combined, calculate the final price under each. ## Online vs. in-store differences

The same retailer can treat online and in-store military discounts differently.

- **Availability.** Some stores only offer the discount in person, or only online.
- **Product range.** Online catalogs may include items that are excluded in store, or the reverse.
- **How it is applied.** In store, the cashier applies it after checking your ID. Online, it is often tied to your account after verification, and you may need to be signed in for it to appear.
- **Returns.** If you return an item bought with a military discount, the refund is usually the amount you actually paid, not the full price. Our guide on [how to check a return policy](/guides/how-to-check-a-return-policy/) has more on this.

If the discount does not show up at online checkout, confirm you are signed in to the verified account before contacting support.

## Protecting your service documents

Military IDs and discharge papers contain sensitive information, and scammers know that veterans and service members are frequent targets.

- **Do not let anyone photocopy or scan your military ID** for a retail discount. Showing it is usually enough. Federal rules generally restrict photocopying of military ID cards.
- **Start from the retailer's official site.** Open the military offer from the store's own website or app, not from a link in an unsolicited email, text, or social media post.
- **Upload the minimum.** If a document upload is required, check whether you can cover your Social Security number or other details that are not needed to confirm service.
- **Be wary of fees.** Legitimate retail military discounts do not charge you to verify.
- **Use strong account security.** Turn on two-step verification for any identity service account you create, since it may be used for more than shopping.

The Federal Trade Commission has guidance on scams that target service members and veterans at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov/).

## Military discount checklist

- Read the store's eligibility list and confirm your group is included.
- Check whether the discount works online, in store, or both.
- Know which ID the store accepts in person, and bring it.
- For online orders, verify through the store's official site and stay signed in at checkout.
- Review the exclusions, especially sale items, appliances, and services.
- Find out if there is an annual savings cap.
- Compare the military discount against any other promotion you cannot stack with it.
- Do not allow your military ID to be copied, and share only the documents you need to.
- Note whether you will need to re-verify later.
