When a coupon code gets rejected at checkout, the code usually isn't broken. More often, something in the cart or the account doesn't meet a condition written into the offer's terms.

Knowing the common restrictions makes it easier to tell whether a code will work before you build a cart around it, and what to do when it doesn't.

## Minimum spend: before or after tax and shipping

Many codes require a minimum order, such as "$20 off orders of $100 or more." The detail that trips people up is what counts toward that $100.

In most cases, the minimum is based on the **merchandise subtotal**, meaning:

- **Before sales tax.** Tax usually doesn't count toward the threshold.
- **Before shipping.** Shipping charges usually don't count either.
- **After other discounts.** If sale prices or another promotion already reduced your subtotal, the reduced amount is often what's measured.
- **Excluding ineligible items.** If some items in your cart are excluded from the offer, they may not count toward the minimum.

**Example:** A code requires a $75 minimum. Your cart shows $82 in merchandise, but $15 of it is a brand the code excludes. The qualifying subtotal is $67, so the code is rejected even though the order total looks high enough.

When the terms are unclear, assume the strictest reading and add a little cushion. To see how codes, tax, and shipping combine into what you actually pay, see our guide to [comparing final prices with shipping and tax](/guides/compare-final-prices-shipping-tax/).

## Excluded brands, categories, and items

Store-wide codes are rarely truly store-wide. Common exclusions include:

- Specific brands, often premium or high-demand ones.
- Electronics, appliances, and other low-margin categories.
- Gift cards, which are almost always excluded.
- New arrivals or limited releases.
- Items sold by third-party marketplace sellers rather than the store itself.
- Services such as installation, protection plans, or subscriptions.

Exclusion lists are usually linked from the offer's fine print as "exclusions apply" or "see details." If a code is rejected and your cart has several items, try removing items one at a time to find which one is ineligible.

## Sale-item exclusions

Many codes say "not valid on sale or clearance items" or "full-price items only." Some stores apply the code to eligible items and skip the rest. Others reject the code entirely if anything in the cart is ineligible.

It's worth comparing the sale price and the coupon price on the same item. Sometimes a code on the full price gives a smaller discount than the existing sale, in which case the coupon doesn't matter. Our guide on [how to tell whether a discount is actually good](/guides/how-to-tell-if-a-discount-is-good/) covers how to judge which price is really better.

## One code per order and stacking rules

"Stacking" means combining discounts. Stores vary a lot here:

- **One promo code per order** is the most common rule. Entering a second code usually replaces the first.
- **Code plus sale price** is often allowed, unless the code excludes sale items.
- **Code plus loyalty rewards or store credit** is sometimes allowed, since these are often treated as a form of payment rather than a discount.
- **Code plus free shipping** depends on the store. Some free-shipping offers count as a promotion and can't be combined with another code; others apply automatically based on order size or membership.
- **Codes plus student, military, or other eligibility discounts** are frequently restricted. Many verified discounts state they can't be combined with other offers. See our guides to [student discounts](/guides/student-discounts-guide/) and [military discounts](/guides/military-discounts-guide/) for how those programs typically work.

When you have more than one code, test each one separately and keep the one that saves the most on your actual cart.

## First-order and new-customer codes

Welcome offers, often given when you sign up for emails or create an account, are usually limited to a customer's first purchase. Stores may check this by email address, account, payment card, phone number, or shipping address.

If you've ordered before, even as a guest or years ago, the code may be rejected. Creating a new account with a different email to get around this is typically against the offer's terms, and stores may cancel orders that appear to do so.

## Single-use, shared, and account-tied codes

Not all codes are meant for everyone.

| Code type | How it usually works |
|---|---|
| Public or shared | One code that anyone can use, often posted in ads, newsletters, or on deal sites. |
| Single-use | A unique code that works once, often issued after a purchase, survey, or sign-up. Once used, it's gone. |
| Account-tied | Linked to a specific customer account or email. It won't work if you're logged into a different account or checking out as a guest. |
| Targeted or personal | Sent to selected customers, such as those with items left in a cart. Usually not transferable even if the code itself is visible. |

If a code that came in your own email doesn't work, check that you're logged into the account tied to that email address.

## Expiration dates and time zones

"Expires 10/31" sounds clear, but there are a few details to check:

- **Time of day.** Many codes expire at midnight, but some end at a specific hour.
- **Time zone.** The cutoff is often based on a particular time zone, such as Eastern or Pacific time, which may not be yours. If you're in a different zone, a code can expire hours earlier or later than you expect.
- **Order time vs. ship time.** The code generally needs to be applied when you place the order, not when it ships.
- **Start dates.** Some codes are announced before they become active.

If you plan to use a code near its deadline, don't wait until the last few minutes.

## In-store vs. online

Some offers are valid only online, only in stores, or only in the store's app. Printed or mobile coupons for in-store use may need to be scanned at the register and may not have a code you can type online. Online-only codes usually can't be redeemed in a physical store. Read which channel the offer covers before making a trip.

## What happens when you return an item

If you used a coupon and return part of the order, the refund often isn't the item's full listed price. Many stores **prorate** the discount, meaning they spread it across the items it applied to.

**Example:** You use a $20-off code on an order with two qualifying items, one $60 and one $40. The store spreads the $20 across them in proportion to price: $12 off the first item and $8 off the second. If you return the $40 item, your refund would be $32, not $40.

Other things that can happen:

- **Losing the minimum.** If the return drops your order below the coupon's minimum, some stores deduct the full discount from your refund.
- **Single-use codes are often not reissued** after a return, even if you return the whole order.
- **Free-gift or BOGO offers** may require returning the free item too, or may reduce your refund by its value.

The return policy usually explains how these cases are handled. See [how to check a return policy](/guides/how-to-check-a-return-policy/) for what to look for before you buy.

## Why codes from random coupon sites often fail

Large coupon aggregator sites list many codes, but plenty of them don't work. Common reasons:

- **Expired codes** that were never removed.
- **Account-tied or single-use codes** that someone posted publicly.
- **Codes for a different country** or a different version of the store.
- **Guessed or recycled codes** listed to fill a page.
- **"Codes" that are really just links** to the store's regular sale page.

The most reliable sources are usually the store itself: its email list, app, account rewards page, and official promotions page. Codes found elsewhere are worth a try, but don't count on them. We explain how we check offers in [how we find deals](/how-we-find-deals/).

## Coupon troubleshooting checklist

If a code doesn't work, go through these:

- [ ] Is the code typed exactly, with no extra spaces, and in the right case if it matters?
- [ ] Is it still active, accounting for time of day and time zone?
- [ ] Does my merchandise subtotal, before tax and shipping and minus excluded items, meet the minimum?
- [ ] Are any brands, categories, sale items, or third-party items in my cart excluded?
- [ ] Is another code or promotion already applied?
- [ ] Is it a first-order code, and have I ordered before?
- [ ] Is it tied to a specific account, and am I logged into that account?
- [ ] Is it valid online, or only in stores or in the app?
- [ ] Would a return later reduce my refund because of how the discount is split?

If everything checks out and the code still fails, contacting customer service with the code and its source can help. Some stores will honor a valid offer manually.
