The price on a product page is rarely what ends up on your card statement. To compare two stores fairly, you need the total after shipping, sales tax, and any fees, which is the number on the final checkout page.

This guide walks through each piece, shows a worked example, and covers the habits that keep a "cheaper" listing from costing more.

## What "landed cost" means

Landed cost is the full amount you pay to get an item to your door. For most online orders, it looks like this:

**Item price − discounts + shipping + fees + sales tax = total**

Two listings for the same item can rank differently once every line is filled in. A store with a higher sticker price and free shipping can easily beat a store with a lower price and a delivery charge.

It is also worth thinking about the cost of sending something back. Paid return shipping or a restocking fee does not show up at checkout, but it can matter if there is a real chance you will return the item. Our guide on [checking a return policy](/guides/how-to-check-a-return-policy/) covers what to look for.

## Shipping costs and free-shipping thresholds

Stores charge for shipping in a few common ways:

- A flat rate per order
- A rate based on weight, size, or delivery speed
- Free shipping once your order reaches a minimum amount
- Free shipping for members of a loyalty or paid program
- Free shipping to a store for pickup

### Padding the cart to reach a threshold

If you are a little short of a free-shipping minimum, adding an item can make sense, but only if you would buy that item anyway.

Here is a hypothetical example. Your cart is $42, the store offers free shipping at $50, and standard shipping is $7.99. Adding an $8 item you already planned to buy saves you $7.99 in shipping. Adding an $8 item you do not need costs you $0.01 more than paying for shipping and leaves you with something you did not want.

Also check what happens if you return part of the order. Some stores deduct the original shipping charge from your refund if a return drops the order below the free-shipping threshold.

## Membership shipping perks vs. paying per order

Paid memberships that include free shipping are worth it when you order often enough. To check, divide the annual fee by the number of orders you realistically place in a year, then compare that to what you usually pay for shipping.

As an example with made-up numbers: a $100 annual membership versus $6.99 per-order shipping breaks even at about 15 orders a year. If you place five orders, you are paying about $20 per order for shipping through the membership.

Keep in mind:

- Membership perks often include more than shipping, so count only the ones you use.
- Many programs offer a free trial. Note the end date so you are not charged for a year you did not plan on. Our [free trials page](/discounts/trial/) lists current trial offers.
- Some retailers offer free shipping with no membership, at a lower threshold, or for specific payment cards. Browse our [shipping offers](/discounts/shipping/) to see what is currently available.

## In-store and curbside pickup

Buying online and picking up at a store usually removes the shipping charge entirely. It can be the cheapest option for heavy or bulky items that carry high delivery fees.

Two things to factor in:

- **Your time and travel.** A long drive can cost more in gas and time than the shipping you saved.
- **Tax location.** Sales tax on pickup orders is generally based on the store's location, which may have a different rate than your home address.

## Sales tax depends on where the order ships

For shipped orders, sales tax is generally calculated based on the delivery address. Rates vary by state and often by county or city, and a few states have no statewide sales tax, though local taxes can still apply in some places. Most large online retailers and marketplaces collect sales tax at checkout.

What this means for comparisons:

- **Enter your real shipping address** before you compare totals. Tax shown with a default or estimated address may be wrong.
- **Tax is usually charged on the price after store discounts**, so a lower item price also means slightly less tax.
- **Whether shipping and certain fees are taxed varies by state.** That can make a small difference between two stores with the same item price.

This is general information, not tax advice. The checkout page is where you see what is actually charged.

## Fees that show up late

Some charges do not appear until the last step of checkout. Watch for:

- Handling fees, sometimes listed separately from shipping
- Oversize, freight, or heavy-item surcharges
- Delivery fees for appliances and furniture, plus separate charges for installation or haul-away of an old item
- Recycling or environmental fees that some states require on certain products, such as some electronics or mattresses
- Rush or scheduled-delivery upgrades
- Service or convenience fees on tickets, travel, and some local services
- Separate shipping charges for each marketplace seller in the same cart

Also look for add-ons that are selected by default, such as protection plans, shipping insurance, or faster delivery. Uncheck anything you did not choose.

## Worked example: Store A vs. Store B

All numbers below are hypothetical, chosen to show the method. Both stores sell the same blender. The example uses a 7% sales tax rate charged on the item price only; real rates and rules depend on your address.

- **Store A** lists the blender at $89.99, with $9.99 shipping (free over $99) or free pickup at a nearby store.
- **Store B** lists it at $94.99 with free shipping.

| Line item | Store A (shipped) | Store A (pickup) | Store B (shipped) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Item price | $89.99 | $89.99 | $94.99 |
| Shipping | $9.99 | $0.00 | $0.00 |
| Fees | $0.00 | $0.00 | $0.00 |
| Sales tax (7%, example) | $6.30 | $6.30 | $6.65 |
| **Total** | **$106.28** | **$96.29** | **$101.64** |

Store A has the lowest sticker price, but shipped to your door it costs $4.64 more than Store B. If you can pick it up, Store A becomes the cheapest by $5.35.

Now suppose you have a 10%-off coupon for Store B. Whether that changes the answer depends on the coupon's terms, such as minimums, excluded brands, or whether it applies to the item only. Our guide to [coupon code restrictions](/guides/coupon-code-restrictions-explained/) explains what to check.

## Check the final checkout page before you decide

Estimates on product and cart pages are often incomplete. To get a real number:

1. Add the item to your cart and go to checkout.
2. Enter your actual shipping address, or choose pickup.
3. Select the shipping speed you would really use.
4. Apply any coupon or promo code.
5. Stop at the review page, before you place the order, and note the total.

Most sites show the final total before you submit. Take a screenshot so you can compare stores side by side and have a record if the price changes.

## Price matching as a factor

Some retailers will match a lower price from certain competitors, or refund the difference if their own price drops shortly after you buy. Policies vary widely, and common conditions include:

- The item must be identical, including model number, size, and color.
- The competitor must be on an approved list, and marketplace sellers are often excluded.
- The item must be in stock at the competitor.
- The match may cover only the item price and not the competitor's shipping costs or membership pricing.

That last point matters. If Store B's total is lower because of free shipping, a price match at Store A might not close the gap. A lower total is a good start, but it is only one part of a good deal. Our guide on [how to tell if a discount is good](/guides/how-to-tell-if-a-discount-is-good/) covers price history and reference prices.

## Final-price comparison checklist

- Compare totals from the final checkout page, not sticker prices or cart subtotals.
- Enter your real address so tax and shipping are accurate.
- Check free-shipping thresholds, and only add items you would buy anyway.
- Do the math on memberships: annual fee divided by your actual orders.
- Price out in-store or curbside pickup when it is convenient.
- Look for handling, oversize, delivery, and recycling fees.
- Uncheck pre-selected add-ons you did not ask for.
- Confirm coupon terms and whether a price match covers shipping.
- Factor in return shipping and restocking fees if a return is possible.
- Screenshot the final total for each store before you decide.
